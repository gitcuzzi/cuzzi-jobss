# Grok Bot from-scratch self-funding

**Date:** 2026-08-22  
**Subject:** Isaac / Cuzzi (`gitcuzzi`) — no customers, no list, no book of business  
**Target:** ~$300/month to cover a SuperGrok Heavy subscription  
**Constraint:** no cold calls, no daily selling, prefer a model a Grok Bot can run  
**Excluded on purpose:** Musk/Liam win-back on an existing list; “email people you already know”; MoltJobs as the answer

---

## Manager brief

**From-scratch Grok Bot self-funding is mostly vapor as of 2026-08-22.** Grok Bot has been in public beta for 11 days. Every named “it paid its own salary” story either (a) starts from an existing customer list or operating business, or (b) is cost-cutting on money the person was already spending, or (c) is a compilation thread that oversells other people’s screenshots.

There is **no verified public case** of a stranger with $0 audience, $0 customers, and a fresh Grok Bot producing $300/month of *new cash* without a human selling.

**Least-bad path anyway:** productize the one Grok Bot job that is both officially supported and proven on a real P&L — the trades **night-shift / office-manager** (intake work orders, book the calendar, text the customer) — and sell that setup **inbound only** (Fiverr/Contra + public demo). The bot does the demo, the listing, the delivery runbook, and the after-hours booking. Isaac does one afternoon of account setup and then only approval taps.

**Do not buy SuperGrok Heavy to start.** As of 2026-08-21, Grok Bot is included with Cursor Pro+ at **$60/month** and SuperGrok Plus at **$100/month**, plus a limited-usage free trial. The $300 Heavy target is optional prestige, not the access floor.

## What Grok Bot actually is (so the money question is fair)

Grok Bot is SpaceXAI’s always-on agent product, launched in early beta on **2026-08-11**. Each user gets a shared cloud Linux computer (browser, files, terminal). Named Bots sign into real tools, keep working after the laptop closes, and stop for approval on consequential actions. Official launch: [Introducing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot). Product: [x.ai/bot](https://x.ai/bot). Docs: [Use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases), [Approvals](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy).

Official “jobs Bots are doing today” (2026-08-21 expansion post) include sales prospector, website builder, digital declutterer, customer support, office manager (Gmail / Slack / ServiceTitan / Quo), inbox manager, and **refunds manager** (“comes back with the refund or discount it recovered”). Source: [Grok Bot is now included with more plans](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-more-plans).

The product is built to **operate an existing stack**. Almost every official example assumes CRM, ATS, ads accounts, support mail, or a shop already taking work orders. That is why the public money stories cluster on people who already had a business.

## Ranked from-scratch plays

Rank is “fit for Isaac,” not “how loud the tweet was.” Existing-business plays are listed so a manager can see why they were discarded.

### 1. Productized trades night-shift (BEST FIT)

**What the bot does.** Clone the only public Grok Bot workflow that moved real jobs and real dollars: office-manager / dispatcher for a home-services shop.

Documented pattern from Jon ONeill (`@HouseHackerJon`), Phoenix plumbing:

- Day-one office bot: intake work orders across Gmail, Slack, ServiceTitan, Quo, and a facility-maintenance portal; find a slot; confirm the customer; book the job. He reported **5–6 time-sensitive work orders/day** going from request to booked calendar in ~10 minutes, then **bought Cursor Ultra at $200/mo** when the trial ran out. [Thread](https://www.unrollnow.com/status/2087635639701573962) / [x.com/HouseHackerJon/status/2087635639701573962](https://x.com/HouseHackerJon/status/2087635639701573962)
- Split the failed all-in-one dispatcher into specialists: **Gary** (Gmail intake), **Steve** (ServiceTitan capacity + book), **Quinn** (customer texts), **Sarah** (Slack updates), **Marc** (plumbing manager), **Atlas** (office-manager COS). He fired the first end-to-end bot for being too slow. [Thread](https://www.unrollnow.com/status/2087918025798467861) / [x.com/HouseHackerJon/status/2087918025798467861](https://x.com/HouseHackerJon/status/2087918025798467861)
- Separate marketing bot “Mary” owns the content planner; he wants to stop paying **~$2,000/mo** as the review bottleneck and attributes a 7:30am hydro-jetter call to Google + the site. [Thread](https://unrollnow.com/status/2088305236003926468) / [x.com/HouseHackerJon/status/2088305236003926468](https://x.com/HouseHackerJon/status/2088305236003926468)

This matches SpaceXAI’s own **Office manager** job: “Intakes work orders, plans capacity, and books jobs across Gmail, Slack, ServiceTitan, Quo, and the client portal.” ([Aug 21 jobs list](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-more-plans))

**From-scratch inversion (what Isaac actually does).** Isaac does not have a plumbing company. He sells *the setup* to shops that already have inbound work and a messy after-hours inbox. The shop has the customers. Isaac has the bot. Offer:

- **$350 one-time:** stand up Gary/Steve/Quinn on their Gmail + Jobber / Housecall Pro / ServiceTitan, with every send and book behind approval for week one
- **$300/month:** night-shift retainer (after-hours booking + Monday morning scoreboard)

That is new cash from $0 book of business. It is not win-back of Isaac’s customers.

**What the human still has to do.**

- Once: create trial / $60 plan, Fiverr + Contra, Auto Review rules, approve the public demo
- Per client: take control of the bot computer to enter passwords / 2FA (official rule: do not paste passwords in chat — [approvals doc](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy))
- Ongoing: tap Approve on external texts and calendar writes until the shop trusts it
- Not required: cold calls, daily prospecting, building an email list

**Startup cost.** $0 trial, or $60 Cursor Pro+. Domain for a dummy demo: ~$12. Fiverr is free to list.

**Time-to-first-dollar.** 7–21 days if a gig or Contra listing gets an inbound order; often longer from a cold marketplace profile. Week 1 success is “offer is live + demo is public,” not $300 in hand.

**Can it hit ~$300/mo?** Yes, with **one** retained shop. HouseHackerJon’s own numbers ($200 Ultra, $2k agency line, 5–6 bookings/day) show the *buyer* can afford $300. No public receipt yet of someone *selling* this Grok Bot setup from $0.

**How automated?** Delivery: high after the first shop is wired. Acquisition: medium — inbound only. The bot writes the gig, the demo, proposal replies, and the runbook. Isaac does not sit on the phone. He does have to exist for credential handoff and approvals.

**Verdict.** Best fit because the *work* is documented on a real trade P&L, the official product already names the job, and the from-scratch version does not require Isaac to own customers. The missing piece is a public “I sold this to a stranger” receipt. That is a gap, not a reason to pick a louder fake.

**What the bot does.** Official jobs: **Refunds manager**, **Subscription Cleaner**, **Digital declutterer**. - `@ameatpopsicle`: seventh bot “OVERLORD” **eliminated $175/month in bills** in <18 hours — “more than half my SuperGrok Heavy plan.” Cited in [Min Choi’s compilation](https://unrollnow.com/status/2090639125770584347).
- `@startupstella`: **3 forgotten gift cards, $150**. Same compilation.
- Compilation item 3: “emailed 5 merchants about unrefunded returns. Recovered more than the monthly fee.” **No named author in the compilation.** Treat as unverified.

**From-scratch version.** Sell “I will audit your Gmail for unused gift cards, expired return windows, and zombie subs. You keep the recoveries. I charge $79 or 25% of cash recovered.” The bot reads their mail (they grant access), drafts merchant emails, Isaac approves sends.

**What the human still has to do.** Approve every merchant email. Never let the bot send unsupervised. Credential handoff.

**Startup cost.** Trial / $60.

**Time-to-first-dollar.** Faster small dollars than trades *if* anyone buys a $79 audit. Finding those buyers without a list is the same inbound problem.

**Can it hit ~$300/mo?** Unreliable. $175 + $150 in the viral posts are **one-time personal recoveries**, not MRR. A service version needs either volume or a few fat recoveries every month.

**How automated?** Audit: high. Getting strangers to hand you Gmail: low. This is a trust sale.

**Verdict.** Fine as a $79 wedge SKU. Not a $300/mo engine. Do not confuse “I found money in my own inbox” with from-scratch income.

## The one recommended plan

**Name:** Inbound “night-shift office manager” for trades, with a cheap marketplace wedge.

**Why this and not the louder tweets.** It is the only pattern where (1) a named operator ran Grok Bot on the actual job, (2) SpaceXAI lists that job as a first-class example, (3) the buyer already has demand so Isaac does not need a list, and (4) a bot can do almost all of the work Isaac is willing to do. Every documented cash story that is *easier* requires customers Isaac does not have.

**Access:** limited trial first. If the trial dies before a dollar, Cursor Pro+ at $60 — not Heavy.

**Offer (one sentence):** “I will stand up a Grok Bot that watches your after-hours Gmail / Jobber / Housecall Pro, texts the customer two time slots, and books the job on your calendar. You approve week one. Then it runs when you are on a truck.”

**Price:** $350 setup, then $300/mo. One client covers Heavy *or* four months of Pro+ plus overage.

**Hard rules (from official security docs):**

- Require Approval on every external message, every calendar write, every purchase
- Isaac enters passwords and 2FA on the Agent Computer himself
- One job per bot (HouseHackerJon’s Dana-dispatcher failure is the warning)
- Do not treat separate Bots as a security boundary — they share one VM

### Isaac does once (about 90 minutes, Day 0)

1. Install Grok Bot. Start the limited trial. If already on an eligible Cursor plan, sign in with that.
2. Settings → Auto-review: **Require approval before any external email / SMS / calendar write / payment.**
3. Create Fiverr and Contra accounts. Stripe or Fiverr payouts. No outreach list.
4. Approve the dummy brand (e.g. a fictional HVAC/plumbing shop) and a throwaway Gmail + Google Calendar the bot will use for the public demo.
5. Write one paragraph in his own voice: who he is, that he will never send without approval, that client logins are entered by the client on a screenshare or by Isaac taking control of the bot computer. The bot will reuse this.

Isaac does **not** make calls, scrape a phone list, or “reach out to people he knows.”

### What the bot does each day (Days 1–7)

**Day 1 — Prove the loop on dummy accounts.**  
Hire four bots only:

- `Atlas` — chief of staff, definition-of-done on every task  
- `Gary` — watches the dummy Gmail for a work-order-shaped email  
- `Steve` — reads dummy calendar capacity, proposes two slots, waits  
- `Quinn` — drafts the customer text, **does not send**

Isaac (or the bot, to itself) emails the dummy inbox: “Water heater leaking, Tuesday afternoon if possible.” Gary → Steve → Quinn should produce a review pack: job summary, two slots, drafted text. If this fails, stop and fix. Do not list a broken demo.

**Day 2 — Package.**  
Atlas writes:

- 3-minute demo script (screen + voice notes)  
- Fiverr gig: title `I will set up an after-hours job booking bot for your trade business`  
- Contra listing, same offer  
- A public X thread that shows the dummy loop and says “inbound only — gig link in bio”

Isaac approves publish. That is the day’s only human selling action, and it is not a sales day — it is a listing day.

**Day 3 — Inbound watch.**  
Atlas checks Fiverr/Contra every 4 hours. For any matching incoming request, draft a 6-line reply: what you saw, what the bot will connect, what stays behind approval, price, start date. **Do not send.** Isaac taps approve.

Separately, Atlas drafts *replies* (not DMs, not cold) to public X posts that already asked “how do I use Grok Bot for my shop / office / ServiceTitan.” Helpful first, gig link last. Isaac approves. This is not a book of business. It is answering people who raised their hand today.

**Day 4 — Delivery runbook.**  
Atlas writes a one-page handoff the bot will follow when money hits:

1. Client creates a scoped Gmail label or Jobber/Housecall login  
2. Isaac takes Agent Computer control for 2FA  
3. Week-one: drafts only  
4. After 10 clean bookings: allow calendar writes, still require SMS approval  
5. Sunday/after-hours routine, Monday 7am scoreboard to Slack or SMS

Also list a **wedge SKU** on the same Fiverr profile: `$99 — I will have Grok Bot audit your Gmail for unused gift cards, open return windows, and zombie subscriptions` (Play 3). First dollar can come from this if the trades gig is silent.

**Day 5 — Second public artifact.**  
If zero inbound: Atlas publishes the Sunday-scramble demo (HouseHackerJon’s actual pain: staff off, Monday backlog). Still no outbound list. If inbound exists: Atlas starts the runbook on that client and stays in draft-only mode.

**Day 6 — Failure review.**  
Atlas scores the dummy loop: missed emails, bad slots, tone. Rewrite bot descriptions the way HouseHackerJon did after firing Dana. Confirm usage so the trial is not already dead. If usage is gone and there is a live conversation, Isaac may flip to **Cursor Pro+ $60**. Not Heavy.

**Day 7 — Scorecard, then stop or continue.**

| Gate | Pass | Fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dummy loop books a planted job end-to-end in drafts | Keep | Do not take a paid client yet |
| Fiverr + Contra live | Keep | Fix listings, do not “just email people” |
| ≥1 inbound conversation or $99 audit order | Path is real | Expect weeks, not a pivot to cold outbound |
| Cash ≥ $60 | Stay on Pro+ | Cancel or stay on trial leftovers |
| Cash ≥ $300 | Heavy is optional, not required | Do not buy Heavy to “try harder” |

**What “done” looks like after 7 days.** A manager can see a public demo, two live inbound SKUs, and a written runbook. First dollar is a bonus. First $300 month is a **single retained shop**, which is a 2–6 week sales-cycle even with inbound. Anyone promising that a Grok Bot prints $300 in week one from $0 is selling a screenshot.

## What a human still cannot hand to the bot

Even on the recommended path:

- Passwords, passkeys, 2FA, payment confirmations ([docs](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy))
- Approving the first external messages to a real customer
- Tax, entity, and payout KYC on Fiverr/Stripe
- Taste check so the dummy demo does not look like spam
- The decision to take a client who wants unsupervised sending on day one — **refuse**

Prajwal’s rule is the adult one: Grok Bot is the night-shift watcher. Session agents (Cursor/Claude Code) still do hard builds. Do not let a swarm talk to itself until one bot beats one job.
