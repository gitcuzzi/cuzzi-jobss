## Four buckets

### (a) Agencies selling bot labor to clients

**Empty on X for Grok Bot.**

What would count: a named account, a client who is not them, a price, and some proof (invoice, Stripe, “this shop pays me $X”). That post does not exist in this window.

The closest *adjacent* sellers:

- **OpenClawPro** ([pricing](https://www.openclawpro.ai/en/pricing), [white-label kit](https://www.openclawpro.ai/en/agency/catalog)): Swiss shop selling managed OpenClaw ($55 / $129 / $219) and a 266-role markdown “AI agency” kit ($149). Self-serve, no sales call. This is the only public “we will run the agent for you” SKU that looks like a product. It is **not on X as a money story**, and it is **not Grok Bot**. Isaac copying it still needs buyers.
- **DeployAgents** ([site](https://www.deployagents.co/)): $14–$55 hosting for OpenClaw/Hermes. Infra, not labor.
- **Higgsfield**: agencies buy Higgsfield and white-label the output. Higgsfield gets paid. The agency already had clients. See Higgsfield section.

Why Grok Bot is a bad multi-client host even if someone wanted (a): official docs say **all Bots share one cloud computer, one cookie jar, one credential pool.** “Do not use separate Bots as a security boundary.” ([approvals doc](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy)). Brandon Charleson said the same in public: **do not use it for agency work yet.** Client A’s ServiceTitan sitting next to client B’s Gmail is a lawsuit, not a retainer.

Prajwal, the only named agency operator in the Grok Bot pile, is the adult in the room: **“I did not let Grok Bot anywhere near client delivery, and I’d tell you not to either.”** ([article via the tweet](https://x.com/PrajwalTomar_/status/2088965811776147761))

## Higgsfield, clipping, Whop — checked, not a Grok Bot agency

**Higgsfield.** Real company. Supercomputer 2.0 is an autonomous marketing agent (NVIDIA Agent Toolkit). CEO Alex Mashrabov’s public line: **roughly 70% of Higgsfield revenue is agencies**, and they have been the buyer from early on. That is Higgsfield selling *to* shops that already have clients — not a stranger standing up Grok Bot and getting paid. ([SaaStr interview writeup](https://cloud.substack.com/p/500m-arr-60-engineers-cash-flow-positive))

The Grok Bot crossover is a **plugin**, not a business. Brock Mesarich shows connecting Higgsfield MCP (86 image/video tools) inside Grok Bot. A Cursor forum bug from Aug 13: Higgsfield MCP **drops every 30–60 seconds**; staff reply: session cannot renew, reconnect right before use, do not expect it to stay live overnight. ([forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-higgsfield-mcp-disconnects-every-30-60-seconds-and-has-to-be-reconnected/168272)) A night-shift creative agency on that connector is fiction until the session holds.

**Clipping / Whop.** Whop clipping (pay-per-1k verified views) and managed clipping agencies are a 2026 industry. CPM guides and “how to make money clipping” YouTube are everywhere. **No named account on X is running that loop on Grok Bot with a payout.** Even if someone did, the human job is daily posting and farm hygiene. Isaac said no daily selling. Bot-detection is also the buyer’s whole problem right now — a Grok Bot farm is how you get banned, not paid.

**Do not** turn Chief of Cuzzi into a clip farm.

## What a human still does (even in the real stories)

From the people who actually ran it:

- Passwords, 2FA, payment confirms — take Agent Computer, never paste in chat ([docs](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy))
- Approve the first external email/SMS/calendar write
- Fire the bot that is too slow (HouseHackerJon fired Dana) and rewrite the job
- Tone (Prajwal: two edits before the night moderator stopped sounding like a hotel)
- Keep session agents (Cursor / Claude Code) for hard builds; Grok Bot is the watcher
- **Not** “set it and forget a client’s books on a shared VM”
